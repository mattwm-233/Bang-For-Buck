# Bang-For-Buck
Are the highest paid athletes in the world worth the money? Which leagues are getting the best deal on their player? I took a look at data from the three major American sports leagues (MLB, NBA, and NFL) to get insight on how much money is spent, and how valuable those assets are.

The ability to measure value in sports varies from league to league. The MLB, for example, has agreed upon value metrics such as WAR (Wins Above Replacement). WAR takes statistics from every aspect of baseball; batting, fielding, pitching, baserunning, and more. These stats are summed together and weighted against position in the field to output the value. The ending value represents the number of wins the player contributes to the team relative to a player of similar skillset. When analyzing data from the NBA and NFL, it isn't as easy as pulling from a single all-encompassing metric. Unlike baseball where the majority of players have to hit and play in the field, the NFL has two distinct sides of the ball with unique statistics tied to specific positions. The NBA combines a few stats together to arrive at metrics like BPM (Box plus-minus) and USG% (Usage rate) that can point to value contributed by a single player.

When comparing leagues, the differences don't stop at individual value. The other aspect of this project is looking at how money is spent in sports. Each of the three leagues is unique in how they handle player salaries and overall team resources. The MLB has been part of recent debates around its lack of a salary cap. With no salary cap, no limit is put on how much money can be spent on building a team. Fans argue that this gives the richest owners/teams the ability to buy success. The other two leagues have a cap in place to comewhat level the playing field. The NBA's cap is labeled as a "soft" cap with a line drawn around $170 million used as a guideline for teams to follow. This number isn't the limit as most teams eclipse that mark, but they then risk falling into the first and second apron which puts restricions on how the team can spend. The NFL has what is know as a "Hard" cap. The cap is set at the beginning of a new seaon and teams cannot spend more than that number on player salaries. These different scenarios change how franchises invest in players. For this project specifically, I chose to focus on how much a player cost on the teams books for the year. For the NBA and NFL that number is represented as Cap Hit, and then AAV (Annual Average Value) for MLB. This allows me to compare how much a player cost their franchise across the different leagues.

# Data
To start collecting the data I need, I will look at contract numbers from 2025. To perface, each league has different time frames where their season takes place. The MLB takes place in a single calender year while the NBA and NFL span 2. With that in mind, I will be working with the salary data and stats from seasons starting in 2025. To pull salary numbers I used *Spotrac* to give me detailed breakdowns of player pay, team totals, and league totals. For the majority of sports statistics and metrics I used *Sports Referance* pages. For more specific value calculations like EPA (Expected Points Added) for the NFL, was gathered from the *nflverse* python package. NBA data was pulled from *databallr* and *Basketball Reference*. 

There are some limitations to mention before diving into the findings. As mentioned before, its not always simple when measuring a players value. For this project we are looking at the top 20 highest paid player in each league. For the NBA and MLB that top 20 is a mix of positions and player types. That is not the case for the NFL where the top 20 is dominated by quarterbacks. The few player that are not quarterbacks include 1 WR (Calvin Ridley), 2 DE/EDGE (Maxx Crosby & DeForest Buckner), and 1 OT (Jawaan Taylor). Because we are using EPA as our value metric for the NFL, we can only evaluate skill players. That means we will be removing Jawaan Taylor, Maxx Crosby, and Deforest Buckner. Salary data is also a challange that needs to be mentioned. Because of the different cap situations, salaries look much different across the leagues. It is difficult to get consistent yealy payroll numbers that make sense to compare. Because of that limitation, I will be using total payroll across the entire league and how much of that total goes to the top 20. With all of that out of the way lets finally start our dive into the data.
